## Engineering Networks / Introduction to Computer Networks

> In this course we will discuss the design of computer networks from a top-down perspective; beginning with applications like email, 
> web browsers and DNS (Domain Name System) all the way down to the signaling protocols used across the cable.  As part of this course, we will 
> design simplified versions of several common network protocols and run them on a network testbed.
>
> Specific topics include: 
>  - introduction (network architectures, protocol layering, TCP/IP and OSI Reference Models)
>  - application layer (Web and http, email, file transfer, DNS)
>  - transport layer (TCP, UDP, connection management, end-to-end reliable data transfer, sliding window protocols,
>                     quality of service, flow control, congestion control, socket programming)
>  - network layer (link-state vs. distance-vector routing, IPv4, IPv6, internetworking)
>  - local area and wireless networks (Ethernet, switches, 802.11)
>  - data link layer (framing, error control, CRC checksums,)
>  - physical layer (analog vs digital transmission, the telephone system, packet vs circuit switching, multiplexing).

Participation is accumulated (rather than subtracted) over the semester and can be awarded through various activities.  Credit will be awarded through in-person lecture and discussion attendance.  It can also be awarded by attending office hours and actively interacting with the teaching staff.

### _Projects_

Projects in this course do not have a formal due date and will not apply a conventional late penalty.  Instead, a multiplier is applied to the submission of each project based on the number of days since it was assigned.  Each project begins with a multiplier of &nbsp;<code>1.10x</code>&nbsp; which decays by &nbsp;<code>0.0125</code>&nbsp; every day.

___Put simply:___ every assignment awards as much as 10% extra credit if turned in on the day it is assigned, full credit if turned in within 8 days, then less each day.

### _Exams_

Exams will cover the material cumulatively throughout the semester.  They will be held on canvas and you may choose to take each exam at any time within the given time window.  These exams are _open notes_ but cooperation between students is NOT allowed as per the university wide policy on cheating.
